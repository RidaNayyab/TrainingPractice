## The Core Idea

Teaching improvement does not happen through inspection. It happens through consistent, specific, well-timed feedback that meets a teacher where she is — and moves her forward one step at a time.

Digital Coach is built on this premise. It observes every lesson, tracks every pattern, and delivers feedback that is focused enough to act on, human enough to trust, and intelligent enough to grow with the teacher.

The measure of whether it is working is **not what the teacher does — it is what students say and show**. Every teaching indicator in this system is connected to a student impact signal. When the teaching improves, the signal changes. That connection is the heart of the system.

---

## Two Observation Modes — One Continuous Picture

Every teacher interacts with Digital Coach in two ways:

**AI-Led Observations** happen every time a teacher self-records a lesson. The AI assesses instructional quality from the recording — clarity, structure, content, questioning, feedback, and more. This forms the daily performance baseline.

**Human-Led Observations** happen periodically when a coach visits in person. The coach uses Digital Coach to observe the full classroom picture — management, environment, resource use, student participation, and the physical dynamics the AI cannot see from a recording.

Together they form one continuous picture. AI observations drive day-to-day growth. Human observations validate, enrich, and catch what AI cannot.

> **Key principle:** AI mastery drives progression through tiers. Human observations never block or reset advancement — they add depth.

## Four Tiers of Development

Teacher growth moves through four progressive tiers. Each tier unlocks the next. No tier is skipped.

### Tier 1 — Structural Foundation

*Can this teacher create the conditions for learning?*

Six indicators — three assessed by AI every lesson, three assessed by humans on visits.

| What AI Observes | What Humans Observe |
|---|---|
| **Instructional Clarity** — students know what they are learning | **Classroom Management** — the environment is safe and productive |
| **Logical Flow** — the lesson has a clear beginning, middle, end | **Productive Learning Time** — time is used well, transitions are smooth |
| **Subject Content Accuracy** — what is taught is correct | **Effective Resource Use** — materials actively support learning |

**Student impact connection:** When structural indicators are strong, students arrive ready, stay attentive, and stay on task throughout. These are preconditions for any learning to happen at all.

**Unlock rule:** AI Structural indicators mastered → Core unlocks. Human Structural gaps flagged on visits, but never gate progression.

---

### Tier 2 — Core Pedagogy

*Is this teacher teaching in a way that builds understanding?*

Core indicators go deeper into how learning is constructed inside the lesson. Universal indicators apply to all subjects.

**Universal Core (AI + Human):**

| Indicator | What It Builds in Students |
|---|---|
| Activities & Tasks Alignment | Students work on tasks that match what they are meant to learn — not busywork |
| Use of Academic Language | Students begin using subject-specific vocabulary themselves |
| Understanding Student Misconceptions | Students' errors are caught early — before they compound |
| Quality Questioning | Students move from recalling facts to explaining reasoning |
| Effective Feedback | Students know what is right, what is wrong, and how to improve |
| Positive & Supportive Disposition | Students feel safe to try, fail, and try again |

**Student impact connection:** When Core indicators are strong, students explain concepts in their own words — not the teacher's. They begin to reason, not just recall. They ask questions, not just answer them.

**Unlock rule:** AI Core substantially mastered (PIC-1 mastered + at least 3 remaining Core indicators consistently strong) → Advanced unlocks.

---

### Tier 3 — Advanced Pedagogy

*Is this teacher transforming how students think?*

Advanced indicators are AI-assessed only. They represent the highest level of instructional practice.

| Indicator | What It Builds in Students |
|---|---|
| Prior Knowledge | Students connect new learning to what they already know |
| Meaningful Connections | Students find personal relevance in abstract content |
| Catering to Learning Levels | Every student — struggling or advanced — is actively supported |
| Responsive Re-explanation | Students who are confused get a new approach, not a repetition |
| Instructional Model Structure (GRR) | Students progressively take ownership of their learning |
| Student Agency & Participation | Students lead, decide, and direct their own learning |

**Student impact connection:** When Advanced indicators are strong, students spontaneously connect lesson content to their own lives and prior learning. They ask genuine questions that push beyond what was taught. Their understanding visibly deepens across a single lesson.

---

### Tier 4 — Subject-Specific Pedagogy

*Is this teacher applying the deepest subject knowledge in how she teaches?*

Subject-Specific indicators activate by subject once Advanced is mastered. They represent the most granular, discipline-specific teaching practices.

#### Mathematics

| Indicator | What It Builds in Students |
|---|---|
| Mathematical Discourse & Reasoning | Students justify their thinking — not just produce answers |
| Problem-Solving & Productive Struggle | Students develop persistence and strategic thinking |
| Concrete / Proxy Materials (Human) | Students grasp abstract concepts through physical manipulation |
| CPA Progression (Human) | Students move fluidly from concrete to pictorial to abstract understanding |

#### Literacy

| Indicator | What It Builds in Students |
|---|---|
| Explicit Phonics / Decoding | Students decode independently — the foundation of all reading |
| Comprehension Strategy Instruction | Students read with active understanding, not passive recognition |
| Reading-Writing Connections | Students transfer literacy skills across modes |
| Interactive Reading & Text Engagement (Human) | Students engage directly with text, not just listen to it |
| Equitable Interactions (Human) | All students participate meaningfully |

#### Science

| Indicator | What It Builds in Students |
|---|---|
| Inquiry-Based Approach | Students ask questions before they receive answers |
| Science Talk & Student Sense-Making | Students verbalise and construct scientific understanding |
| Hands-On / Minds-On Participation (Human) | Students actively investigate, not just observe |
| Concrete Anchoring of Scientific Concepts (Human) | Students ground abstract science in observable reality |

**Student impact connection:** When Subject-Specific indicators are strong, students use the language and logic of the discipline itself. A Maths student reasons like a mathematician. A Literacy student reads and writes with strategy. A Science student questions and investigates like a scientist.

> *No further tier unlocks. Subject-Specific is the final stage of the development journey.*

## How Student Impact Is Measured

Every teaching indicator is connected to a student signal. These signals — called **Student Learning Gain Proxies (AI-led)** and **Student Engagement Markers (human-led)** — are never separate feedback items. They are evidence that teaching is working.

They operate in two ways:

### 1. As the "why it matters" behind every feedback point

Every time a teacher receives feedback on an indicator, the connection to student impact is embedded in the language:

- *"When your lesson goal is clear from the start, students find their own words for it — not just yours"* (SLGP-1 → student conceptual expression)
- *"When your questions go deeper, students start generating their own — and that is when real thinking begins"* (SLGP-3 → student-initiated questions)
- *"When you re-explain using a new approach, students who were lost catch up — and you can see it in how they respond"* (SLGP-5 → visible learning progression)

### 2. As tone amplifiers on flagged indicators

When a teacher indicator is flagged, the student signal tells the system how urgently to frame the feedback:

| Teaching Signal | Student Signal | Feedback Tone |
|---|---|---|
| Indicator flagged | Students are not showing impact | More urgent — *"students are not yet showing this"* |
| Indicator flagged | Students showing impact despite the gap | Gentler — *"Students are responding well; this just needs consistency"* |
| Indicator strong | Students showing strong impact | Full celebration |
| Indicator strong | Students are not showing impact | Coach dashboard alert — warrants closer observation |

## How Feedback Is Generated

### One Report Per Day

Whether a teacher records one lesson or eight, she receives **one feedback report at the end of the day**. Not one per class. One.

### The 3-Item Cap

No matter how many gaps are identified, feedback surfaces a **maximum of three improvement areas**. Research on behaviour change is clear:
- 4+ items → implementation drops to 30%
- 3 items → implementation holds at 60%

Focus is more powerful than comprehensiveness.

### Priority Matrix

When more than three gaps exist, the system selects which three to surface:

1. **P1** — Critical gaps always first (foundation blockers)
2. **P2** — High impact gaps next (lower tier before higher tier)
3. **P3** — Moderate gaps only when all P1s and P2s are resolved
4. **Cap at 3** — hold the rest for the next cycle

### Multi-Class Days

When a teacher records multiple classes in one day, the system consolidates:

- **Gap in the majority of classes** (3+ out of 5, or 2 out of 2–3) → teacher-level habit, flagged
- **Gap in one subject or grade only** → named specifically in feedback, not treated as a teacher-wide habit
- **One flag per day**, regardless of how many classes showed the gap

### Escalation Logic

The type of support changes as a gap persists:

| Flag Count | Support Type | Tone |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | Text — clear, practical tip | Instructional |
| 2nd | Text — more specific, diagnostic | Supportive |
| 3rd | Text + video resource | Encouraging |
| 4th | New strategy + same video + coaching alert | Intensified |
| 5th+ | Minimal text + 1:1 coaching intervention | Problem-solving |

> Human observations carry the AI flag history forward. A teacher at the 4th flag does **not** get stepped down to the 1st flag because a coach visited.

## The Validation Loop

Teachers need to feel heard. Data across surveys, interviews, and platform analysis shows this consistently — **41% sometimes disagree with feedback**, and when there is no outlet, scepticism becomes the only response.

The validation loop gives teachers a voice — in three steps, rolled out incrementally:

### Step 1 — Pre-Observation Context (Phase 1)

Before recording starts, teacher taps one of three options:

- 🏫 Large class / limited resources (from girls/boys strength data can be fetched)
- 📚 Mid-unit lesson
- ✅ All good

Context is passed into feedback generation before the AI assesses anything. Feedback opens with acknowledgment of what was flagged.

### Step 2 — Post-Feedback Response (Phase 1 + Phase 2)

After reading feedback, before closing:

- *"Did this feel right?"* → 👍 or 👎
- If 👎 → open text box: *"What didn't feel right?"*

In **Phase 2**, each flagged item gets its own yes/no toggle. If no → text box appears for that item specifically.

### Step 3 — Real-Time Feedback Revision (Phase 3)

Teacher submits input → system reads the original observation data alongside the teacher's text → generates revised feedback immediately:

> *"Thank you for sharing that — here is your revised feedback."*

Teacher input **never removes a growth area**. It adjusts framing, offers alternative strategies, and flags context for the coach. If the same item is disputed across 3+ cycles with no improvement, the coach is alerted for a direct conversation.

## What the Coach Sees

The coach dashboard is a backend intelligence tool — **never visible to teachers**. It shows:

- AI indicator scores and trends per teacher
- Flag counts and escalation status per indicator
- Student signal health (SLGPs and Section D markers)
- Teacher validation responses — original feedback, teacher input, revised feedback
- **Alerts:**
  - 3rd flag → coaching alert
  - 5th flag → 1:1 intervention needed
  - 3+ disputed cycles → direct conversation needed

The dashboard gives coaches the full picture before every human visit — so the observation is **diagnostic and purposeful**, not a repeat of what the AI already knows.

## What This System Is Building

- **At the structural tier**, it builds safe, organised, well-paced classrooms.
- **At the core tier**, it builds teachers who can construct understanding — not just deliver content.
- **At the advanced tier**, it builds teachers who can differentiate, respond, and hand ownership to students.
- **At the subject-specific tier**, it builds teachers who think like mathematicians, literacy experts, and scientists.

And running underneath all of it — the **student signals**. Because the goal was never to change what teachers do. It was always to change what students experience.
